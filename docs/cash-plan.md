# Cash plan

B09 in the [agent-first build plan](agent-first-build-plan.md). This page records what is built: the base 13-week plan, named what-if scenarios, reviewed versions that changed inputs invalidate, and comparing a reviewed plan with what happened.

## The base plan

A client close's **Cash plan** tab (`GET /v1/closes/{id}/cash-plan`, MCP `cash_plan`) lays the client's next 13 weeks of book cash out day by day, with no model call and no estimate of its own:

- **Opening book cash:** the home-currency bank accounts' balances in the close's newest QuickBooks capture, as of that capture. It is the ledger's cash, never called bank-available cash; accounts in another currency are left out and named. The plan starts on the capture's day.
- **Receipts:** the latest capture's open invoices, on the date a customer promised (recorded under [Follow up open invoices and bills](work-inbox.md#cash-operations-overdue-receivables-and-payables)) or otherwise the due date.
- **Payments:** open bills, on the date the client's bill-pay tool will pay (a recorded handoff) or otherwise the due date.
- **Schedules:** payroll, tax, debt service, rent and other commitments from the client's own schedules (`GET`/`POST /v1/closes/{id}/cash-obligations`, migration `109_cash_obligations.sql`): once, weekly, every two weeks or monthly (on the same day, or the month's last day). Each is kept in the client's home currency when it is added (one in another currency is left out and named), counts only after a person with `workpapers:review` approves it, and starts counting from the plan's first day (earlier dates are already in the opening balance); a change retires it with a reason and adds a new one. Nothing runs payroll or files tax. How a bill relates to a schedule's date is a person's decision, never inferred from a vendor and a week; see [Bills and schedules](#bills-and-schedules).

Each week shows its opening (last week's closing), receipts, payments, closing and its **lowest end-of-day balance** with the date, so a Wednesday payroll shortfall shows even when Thursday's receipts leave Friday positive. The lowest projected end-of-day book cash across the 13 weeks, with its date and the horizon's last day, leads the page. Arithmetic is exact on decimal strings, and each week's totals are the sums of the items listed under it.

Receipts are only what is already invoiced, plus any approved receipt schedule (a schedule can be a receipt, for example a retainer or expected monthly sales): later weeks show what is already known, not a sales forecast, and the page says so. Nothing is given a date nobody agreed: a past-due invoice or bill without a promise or handoff, a disputed or on-hold item, one with no due date and one with an unreadable balance are listed apart with the reason, as are items after the 13 weeks and in another currency. The bank balances and the open items must come from the same QuickBooks company. A capture holds invoices and bills dated up to its close's month-end, so when the plan starts after that date it says later-dated items are missing and points to the latest month's close. The plan says when the latest capture did not read every open item, when the bank balances and the open items were read more than a day apart, and when the balances' capture is no longer current. A Xero client's plan is not built yet.

**Checks:** `TestPlanWeeksShowsAMidweekShortfallAndCarriesBalances` (hand-worked weeks, a midweek low, items outside the horizon) and `TestCashPlanFromTheLedgerOpenItemsAndApprovedObligations` (opening from the capture, a promised date, two-weekly payroll, the rent's bill asked about and then decided, a disputed bill and a past-due invoice left unscheduled, a broken promise, an unapproved obligation not counted, every week reconciled to its items).

## Bills and schedules

A schedule that names its vendor can be paid by that vendor's bills, but a bill from the landlord in the week rent is due may be the rent, part of it, or something else (a $500 maintenance bill). The plan never decides that from the vendor and the week. Someone with `workpapers:review` decides it once per bill and schedule date (`POST /v1/closes/{id}/cash-matches`, migration `150_cash_obligation_matches.sql`):

- **Pays all of it** (`replaces`): the bill is that date's payment, so the schedule's estimate for the date is not counted. The bill counts on its own date, which may move (a bill-pay handoff to a later week) without losing the link.
- **Invoices part of it** (`part`), with the amount it invoices: the schedule's amount less what its bills invoice still counts as a residual. A $4,000 bill against $10,000 rent counts the bill and $6,000. A credit or partial payment later applied to the bill lowers what the bill pays, not what is still to be invoiced.
- **A separate payment** (`separate`): both count.
- **Repeats a payment already counted** (`duplicate`): the bill is left out of the weeks under that decision (the same rent bill entered twice).

A bill decided against a date that the plan does not count (disputed, on hold, undated or past due) does not stand in for the payment, so the schedule's estimate counts for it and the plan says why. A bill that leaves the open items was paid, voided or credited, and no capture says which: the estimate counts again until someone confirms it was paid (**It was paid**), after which it settles what it was decided to pay. A missing bill says anything only in a capture that read every open item, was taken after the decision and covers the bill's own date; otherwise the plan says the capture cannot tell. A bill pays or repeats at most one date. A new decision for the same bill and date must name the one it replaces, so two people never overwrite each other unseen; earlier decisions are kept. A paid bill's decision can be changed but not cleared. Retiring a schedule retires the decisions made against it, so its bills are asked about again against whatever schedule applies now; a decision whose date stops counting any other way is named under readiness and can be cleared.

Until someone decides, an open bill from the schedule's vendor that falls in the date's week, by due date or payment date, is a **question**, even when another bill already pays the date: both the bill and the schedule count, so the payment may be counted twice, and the page says by how much that week's payments fall if the bill pays all of it. A plan with an open question, or a bill waiting to be confirmed paid, cannot be reviewed. A review is **current** only for a complete forecast: a draft or incomplete one can be reviewed, and its review says **Reviewed as a draft**, with the unresolved items it accepted kept in it. A decision names the capture the person saw, and is refused once the books are captured again. Another vendor's bill, a bill left out of the weeks, or a vendor credit is never asked about.

## Readiness

The plan states whether it is a conclusion. It is **incomplete** while a source is missing: a stale or partial capture, balances and open items read far apart, later-dated items not captured, a bank balance or an item's date or balance that could not be read, or a decided bill the capture cannot account for. Items that need an agreed date (past due, a broken promise, undated, disputed or on hold) or are left out by design (another currency) are assumptions, listed as decisions. A scenario whose move could not be applied (the item has since been paid, disputed or left undated) is incomplete too. Otherwise it is a **draft** while a decision is open: a bill question, a bill to confirm as paid, or a proposed schedule not yet approved (a bonus suggested from last year's pattern is a proposal, never a commitment). Each unresolved item is named with its amount under the headline. The plan shows its horizon, as-of time and whether its newest review is still current or has changed since.

The agent reads the same plan: a figure from a draft or incomplete plan must be stated as such.

**Checks:** `TestSettleOccurrenceCountsOnlyWhatDecisionsSay` (rent with a separate maintenance bill, a partial bill and its residual with a credit and after payment, two schedules for one vendor, full replacement, a duplicate, disputed and undated bills, a moved payment date, bills beyond the schedule, no question for another vendor, a credit or another week), `TestCashReadinessNamesWhatIsUnresolved`, and the decision part of `TestCashPlanFromTheLedgerOpenItemsAndApprovedObligations` (refusals, a decision replacing another only when it names it, a bill paying one date only, a review refused while a question is open, a review current and then changed).

## What-if scenarios

Under **What if** on the plan, anyone who can open it (including a client's executive) can try a named scenario (`GET`/`POST /v1/closes/{id}/cash-scenarios`, migration `115_cash_scenarios.sql`): move an open invoice or bill to another date (a customer paying late, a bill paid early), or add a receipt or payment once or weekly, every two weeks or monthly up to an optional last date (a new hire's pay, a one-off purchase). `GET /v1/closes/{id}/cash-plan?scenario={id}` lays the weeks out with those changes and reports the plan's own lowest point beside the scenario's, so a delayed receipt shows exactly which day turns negative.

A scenario is kept with the QuickBooks books and home currency it was made for and is not applied to other books or after the home currency changes, since another company could give the same document key to a different invoice. A client's executive sees, opens and removes only their own scenarios; the firm's what-ifs (a draft salary, say) stay with the firm, while the firm sees every scenario of the client. A scenario that can no longer be shown falls back to the plan as it stands, with the reason.

A scenario changes only that view: never the base plan, a promise or bill-pay date recorded on an open item, an approved schedule or the ledger. It keeps the open items it moves by their document (`invoice:3`); one that has since been paid, disputed or left undated is named as not applied. Removing a scenario keeps its record.

**Checks:** `TestScenariosMoveOpenItemsAndAddPaymentsWithoutTouchingTheBase` (a moved copy, a two-weekly payment up to its end date, a move with nothing to move, refused changes) and the scenario part of `TestCashPlanFromTheLedgerOpenItemsAndApprovedObligations` (a delayed promised invoice and a hire through the API, the base plan unchanged, an executive seeing only their own scenario, a scenario for other books refused, a removed scenario no longer shown).

## Reviewed versions and forecast against actual

Until a person reviews it, the plan is a working forecast. Under **Reviewed versions**, someone with `workpapers:review` reviews the plan in view (as it stands, or with a scenario) by stating the assumptions they accept, the dates, promises and schedules relied on, and confirming the figures they saw (`POST /v1/closes/{id}/cash-plan/reviews`, migration `116_cash_plan_reviews.sql`). The plan's digest covers every figure and date shown, the capture and open-items read they came from, and the calculation (`cash_plan_v1`); each item names the decision or approved schedule that dates or adds it. A review is refused if the plan changed since they looked, its capture is no longer current, its open items were not all read, or the newest review of that view already covers exactly this plan (a plan that returns to an earlier state can be reviewed again). Reviews of a close are made one at a time. The reviewed plan is kept whole with the assumptions; rows are only ever added.

`GET /v1/closes/{id}/cash-plan/reviews` lists reviews newest first. The newest review of each view is **current** only while the plan can still be laid out from a current, complete capture with the same figures and sources; it says **inputs changed** once a capture (even with the same totals), a promise, a bill-pay date, a schedule or a scenario changes it, or the books can no longer be read; the review itself stays as it was, and older ones are superseded. Each reviewed plan is compared with every later QuickBooks capture of the same books and currency within its 13 weeks (up to 30): the book cash at the capture's time beside the balance the reviewed plan expected at the end of that day, and the difference. A capture can be earlier in the day than that day's planned payments and receipts, so the page shows its time and says so. A home-currency bank balance that cannot be read is named and no actual is stated for that capture. Amounts are shown in each review's own currency. A client's executive sees reviews of the plan as it stands, not of scenarios, and cannot review.

**Checks:** the review part of `TestCashPlanFromTheLedgerOpenItemsAndApprovedObligations` (a short statement refused, a stale digest refused, a review, a repeat refused, current with nothing to compare; after a bill-pay handoff it shows inputs changed, and a capture three days later is compared: forecast -500.00, actual 5,000.00, difference 5,500.00; a capture with an unreadable bank balance states no actual), and the executive checks in `TestExecutivesReadOnlyTheirClientsPublishedReportsAndCashViews`.
