# Demonstration seed: a firm with four months of history

`cmd/seed` fills one firm with the clients, months, documents, agent work and decisions a small accounting practice would have after four months on Krith, so the workspace can be seen with real relationships in it rather than empty states. The data is synthetic; the paths are not. Everything a person does is written through the routes they use (opening months, planning, confirming the agent's work, preparing drafts from linked evidence, approving, deciding coverage, scoping, signing off, publishing, budgets, cash plans, open items). The month-end books are written the way the acceptance run writes a capture (`quickbooks.PeriodSnapshot` from generated QuickBooks-shaped reports, a transaction capture with its rows, open items and exceptions), and each document is read by the pipeline with a recorded answer, as `docs/acceptance-corpus.md` describes.

## The firm

Ten clients, taken on in June 2026; October 2026 is the running month, so September is the month being closed.

| Client | Business | Recurring work | Where September stands |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harbor Fixture LLC | Lighting wholesaler | Two prepaids (software, insurance); budget; handed to the agent | Drafts wait for approval |
| Bluegrass Dental PLLC | Dental practice | Gusto payroll, Riverbend equipment note, prepaid software; every month handed to the agent | A request for the loan statement is approved and waiting to be sent |
| Cedar & Pine Coffee Co. | Two cafés | Bank reconciliation from the statement export and the captured register; owner items | Signed off |
| Larkspur Landscaping Inc. | Commercial landscaping | Deere truck note, prepaid insurance, late-paying customers, cash plan, open items | Overdue (due five days after month-end), with a retainage question and an accrual open |
| Meridian Physical Therapy PC | Physical therapy | ADP payroll, prepaid malpractice insurance | Signed off, then ADP reissued the payroll summary with higher employer taxes: the sign-off no longer stands |
| Orchard Street Books LLC | Bookstore | Prepaid point-of-sale, August utilities accrual | Documents in, no books captured |
| Summit Peak Fitness LLC | Fitness studio | Gusto payroll, monthly utilities accrual with reversal, prepaid insurance, budget, cash plan; packs published for the owner | Signed off and published |
| Tidewater Marine Services Inc. | Boat yard | Riverbend note with accrued interest, prepaid insurance | A scan the agent could not read; drafts wait for approval |
| Juniper Design Studio | Design studio | On the roster since September | Owed, not opened |
| Northgate Auto Repair | Auto repair | Prepaid insurance | Left in August; June and July signed off, roster inactive |

June, July and August are signed off for every client that was there, with coverage decided for every account, the opening baseline reviewed at takeover, the carried schedules refreshed against each month's capture, time recorded, and the next month opened by sign-off as the product does it. The firm's policies are set with their reasons (agent on firm-wide, materiality, link passcodes, reminders), the monthly template is due on the 15th, each client's procedures are published (its basis, and the payroll, loan, owner, accrual, reconciliation and prepaid rules it uses; nothing firm-wide, so a real client's defaults are untouched; no prepaid method is recorded, since that takes the reviewed procedure-learning path, so the agent asks the accountant to confirm each prepaid reading), and there are invitations, agent access for the administrator, chats with the agent, inbox marks and daily book reviews.

The books are generated by `internal/seed`: a shared chart with each business's accounts, opening balances, recurring rent, utilities, software, supplies, advertising, customer invoices and payments, the business's own purchases and bills, and what the work describes (the prepaid bill posted to expense, the loan payment, the payroll debits, the owner's personal charge, the accrual's reversal and actual bill). Each month's capture is taken before the firm's entries are posted, as the product captures a real month; once the month is signed off, its approved entries are posted by the client's bookkeeper dated month-end, so the next month opens on the captured closing plus them, and each month's Trial Balance ties to the schedules the way a real client's does. The next close holds a readback of those postings ("QuickBooks entries posted for …"), separate from its own month's transaction capture, so the lineage from signed draft to opening balance is on record; every capture's period reports and transaction capture certify against each other. `go test ./internal/seed` checks that every client-month balances, ties its cash flow to the bank accounts, and reads back through the capture readers.

## Running it

A local firm, on a migrated local database (`docker compose up -d postgres`, a disposable database, `infra/postgres/migrate.sh`):

```sh
cd services/backend
go run ./cmd/seed -database "$DATABASE_URL" -organization local-development -subject local-development -members user_demo_rosa
```

`-members` names other current members to own some clients: they are assigned those clients, and whether they can review their work depends on their own role. Without it every close is the administrator's.

The hosted firm, from your own terminal with the owner connection string (the one Deploy production migrates with). The seed refuses a database that is not on this machine unless `-hosted` is given, and then needs `-yes`:

```sh
go run ./cmd/seed -database "$MIGRATION_DATABASE_URL" -domain getkrith.com -email you@getkrith.com -hosted -yes
```

`-domain` finds the firm registered under the address domain; `-email` finds your subject from your sign-ins (or pass `-subject`). The run takes well under a minute and prints each refused step as a problem; a clean run ends with `Done with 0 problems`.

To take it out again, keeping the firm and its members:

```sh
go run ./cmd/seed -database "$MIGRATION_DATABASE_URL" -domain getkrith.com -hosted -yes -remove
```

The seed records what it did in `demonstration_seeds` (migration 140): who ran it, the firm's policy as it found it, and, as each is made, the IDs of the closes, chats, inbox marks, invitations, agent access and procedures it added. A run that stops partway is removed the same way. Rows made under a demonstration client's name after the run (a month someone opened, a budget they added) are theirs and stay; a later seed then refuses until they are gone. The seed writes no usage of its own; the model calls the pipeline counted while reading the documents stay as usage, since they happened. Removal refuses while a legal hold or a retention date still to come protects any demonstration close, receipts each deletion as the deletion route does, and deletes exactly those rows, with the demonstration clients' roster entries, aliases, budgets, obligations, scenarios, open-item decisions and connections, and sets the firm's policy back to what it was (recorded as one more policy change by the person who ran the seed; the policy history itself stays; a member's own conversation that mentions a demonstration close keeps its messages, detached from it), and takes out the time zone and notification preferences it set for the administrator when they had none. Seeding again afterwards works.

## What the seed will not do

- **Touch a firm that holds work under its client names.** It refuses when the firm already holds the demonstration, or has a close, roster entry or alias for any of its ten client names, so removal can never reach a real client's work.
- **Change who may enter the firm.** The administrator and every `-members` subject must already be current members; the seed grants membership only in a firm it created itself (a local database).
- **Create a hosted firm, or act for someone the firm does not call an administrator.** A hosted run needs an existing firm, and its own records must show the person as an administrator (its creator or first member, someone given the firm-admin role, or someone enrolled by a firm-admin invitation).
- **Act as anyone but the person who runs it.** Every decision it records (confirmations, approvals, coverage, sign-offs) is the administrator's; other members only own closes and have their time recorded. A firm that requires an independent reviewer is refused, since the seed cannot give it one honestly. The rest of the policy it changes (agent, materiality, passcodes, reminders) is restored on removal, field by field, where the firm has left it as the seed set it; once the firm has recorded any policy change of its own after the run, nothing is put back. A firm with its own monthly template keeps it; one without gets a 15-day template, which stays after removal (a template, once set, has a due date), to be changed under Settings → Clients.
- **Work another firm's queue.** The pipeline steps it runs take only this firm's jobs; everything else is the worker's.
- **Run against a database it cannot place.** The connection string is read as the driver reads it (a URL or `key=value` pairs); anything that is not localhost, a loopback address or a socket path needs `-hosted -yes`.

## What to know before seeding the hosted firm

- **The worker is running there.** The seed works the queue itself, with recorded answers, and the production worker may claim some of the same intake jobs and read them with the live model instead. The seed reports any document whose reading did not land where the story expects; rerunning the seed after `-remove` is the fix.
- **Nothing reaches a provider.** The QuickBooks connections are sandbox connections labeled as the demonstration's (their company name says "demonstration books" and their realm is none QuickBooks issues), so nothing they hold counts as a live provider read, and they hold no credential, so no refresh is scheduled; nothing is posted; no email is sent (the firm has no sender connected); invitations are rows, not WorkOS invitations. People are shown by their WorkOS profile, so other members' closes read as "held by a colleague" when their subject is not a WorkOS user of the firm.
- **It is one story.** The seed runs once per firm; a second run is refused until the first is removed.
- **The goals are live.** Harbor's hand-over and Bluegrass's monthly routine are real goals the worker will look at and report on.

`SEED_TRACE=1` prints every request the seed makes and what the route answered.
