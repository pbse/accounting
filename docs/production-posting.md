# Controlled production QuickBooks posting

Production posting is a separate, manually activated capability. Ordinary local development needs no production credentials and never calls a production QuickBooks company. The supported writes are one USD journal per approved draft: the initial full-month **prepaid**, and, once enabled (below), an **accrual**'s month-end adjustment (never its next-month reversal), a **loan** schedule's month-end entries combined in one journal, and an answered **owner** item's reclassification. Blocked, question-only and other drafts are never posted.

The same controls apply to Xero manual journals; see [Xero posting](xero-onboarding.md#approved-journal-posting). A draft, or another draft of its source, reaches at most one of the two ledgers.

**Rule: agents prepare, people approve before anything is written.** Agents may prepare drafts, comparisons and proposed entries, and may read from QuickBooks and Xero. Every write to an outside system needs a person's approval first: a QuickBooks journal, Bill or line recode, payment or credit application, or supplier credit, a Xero manual journal or bill line recode (`posting:approve`, then `posting:execute`), or a client email (each message, including every reminder; the firm's reminder rule only says when one is due). No background worker writes to QuickBooks or Xero or sends email on its own, and no agent tool can post.

## Setup

1. Back up PostgreSQL. Stop API and worker, apply `023_production_posting.sql` after 022, then deploy matching code. Fresh Compose volumes apply all migrations. The API readiness check requires the new tables.
2. Create an Intuit **production** app separately from the sandbox app. Register the exact HTTPS API callback `https://YOUR_API_HOST/v1/quickbooks/callback`. Set server-only `QBO_PRODUCTION_CLIENT_ID`, `QBO_PRODUCTION_CLIENT_SECRET`, `QBO_PRODUCTION_REDIRECT_URI`, `PUBLIC_APP_URL` and the existing `SECRETS_KEY` on the API and the worker (the worker refreshes production connections and captures their transactions daily). Leave these blank until activation. GitHub staging accepts the ID and redirect as variables and the secret as a secret; it rejects partial configuration. A production release workflow and environment are not included in staging.
3. In WorkOS, give only authorized client accountants `workpapers:review` and `posting:approve`, and authorized human executors `posting:execute`. Grant those users the exact client via client access; `clients:all` is firm-wide access. A user connecting Intuit also needs `integrations:manage`. No agent receives these permissions. Verify roles and client assignments before connecting a live company.
4. Open the client close and **Production QuickBooks authorization and client grant**. Connect, complete OAuth, and confirm that the QuickBooks company name exactly equals the client name on the close. Names that differ are blocked; reconcile the identity before posting. The production OAuth connection and credentials are separate from sandbox evidence capture and sandbox posting.
5. With `posting:approve`, enter the production Chart of Accounts IDs the grant allows (2–20), the draft kinds it covers, a USD maximum per journal total, an expiry within 30 days and the reason. Review the exact company and client, then type `GRANT PRODUCTION POSTING`. Each proposal account name must match exactly one allowed account by name; otherwise preview refuses before any write. Grants made before migration 046 cover prepaid drafts only. A current grant can be revoked immediately.

## Per-workpaper flow

Approve the saved workpaper with current source evidence first. In its **Reviewed production posting** panel, prepare a preview. The API checks the supported method, client and close month, active company, exact account names/classifications/currency, positive amount and grant cap. It captures each account's exact `CurrentBalance` and `SyncToken` as ledger preconditions. These are **current** balances, not an atomic period-end Trial Balance. Inspect them against the client's ledger and other postings.

**One approval per entry.** A preview needs no prior draft approval. If the draft is still pending when a person with both `posting:approve` and `workpapers:review` approves the posting, that one action also approves the draft, recorded in its review history as "Approved together with its posting." Both are saved in the same transaction. The usual review checks apply: current evidence, no blockers, the accounting basis and the firm's independence rule. A person without `workpapers:review`, or a draft that needs changes or whose sources changed, still needs a separate review. Execution is unchanged and always needs a fresh posting approval. The sandbox posting panel works the same way. Supplier credits and Bill corrections keep their existing steps.

Type `APPROVE PRODUCTION DRAFT` to bind approval to the preview. It expires after ten minutes. Type `POST TO PRODUCTION` to execute. Immediately before the write, the API re-reads the company and accounts; any change in the observed preconditions, workpaper review, grant or source version blocks the write. A newer draft of the same source blocks it too; this is checked again under the close lock in the same transaction that records the attempt, so a draft saved while the decision waits cannot slip past. It commits the unique intent/request ID and `executing` attempt before calling Intuit. It never automatically retries the write, including after a timeout or lost response.

After Intuit returns a journal ID, the API reads that journal by ID and verifies its date, currency, note, two account IDs, posting directions and exact amounts. It then re-reads both current account balances and requires the observed prepaid debit and expense credit effect. The saved receipt contains the journal readback and account observations. If an ID or balance readback is missing or differs, state remains `unknown`; use **Recheck saved journal and balances** after investigating QuickBooks. Report lag or concurrent ledger activity can leave this state unresolved even if the journal exists. No second write button appears. If no external ID was returned, investigate the displayed provider request ID directly in QuickBooks. Enter a found journal ID in the posting panel; the server binds it only after its canonical fields match the saved intent, then checks the account effect. A wrong ID never authorizes a second write. Reconnecting the same exact production company permits a later readback, including after a grant expires or is revoked. Once verified, a later journal identity recheck does not reinterpret balances changed by subsequent activity.

The close readiness digest and review ZIP include production attempt states and stored receipts. `executing` or `unknown` blocks sign-off. Export is a saved snapshot, not a new provider read.

The close page's **Close ledger rehearsal** lists saved, unexecuted production journal previews and combines their effects by account against one versioned balance. It requires the same QuickBooks company, connection, account ID, SyncToken and exact balance across every contributing intent. A different snapshot, more than 100 open proposals, or an uncertain execution blocks the combined result; workpapers needing accountant review remain identified. The preview is one read-only database snapshot, not a fresh QuickBooks query or an official trial balance. It excludes sandbox Bill/VendorCredit corrections and already verified postings. Refresh each individual preview before approval or posting; the server rechecks live preconditions at execution. Overlapping approved intents may still block individual posting even when their combined arithmetic can be rehearsed.

## Local and staged validation

Run `npm ci`, `npm run check --workspace @close/frontend`, and the focused Go tests under `services/backend`. CI applies every migration to PostgreSQL and runs the integration tests with synthetic credentials and mocked Intuit HTTP. Do not configure a real production token for tests. Before real activation, validate the Intuit app, WorkOS roles, account IDs, company identity, grant limits, book-close policy and provider permissions with the client's accounting professional. This code cannot make QuickBooks reads and writes atomic with other users' activity.

Intuit references: [QuickBooks API environment URLs](https://developer.intuit.com/app/developer/qbo/docs/api/accounting/all-entities/invoice), [idempotent request IDs](https://blogs.a.intuit.com/2018/09/10/quickbooks-online-api-best-practices/).

## Closing date and independent review

A signed-off close takes no journal decision: approving or executing a sandbox, production or Xero journal is refused under the close's lock until the close is reopened. Reading back an earlier attempt still works. An executing or unknown Xero journal blocks sign-off, as production and sandbox attempts already did, so a Xero write in flight cannot be overtaken by a sign-off.

Every QuickBooks write (production and sandbox JournalEntry, sandbox VendorCredit and Bill correction) reads the company's `AccountingInfoPrefs.BookCloseDate` first and refuses a transaction dated on or before it, before any attempt is reserved. An unreadable or malformed closing date also blocks the write; an empty date means the company has no closing date. The production preview records the closing date, so a change between preview and execution rejects the stale intent without writing. Readback after a write does not recheck the closing date, so books closed after a successful write still verify.

Firms can require independent review (Firm settings, `org:manage`, hosted firms only; migration 040). With it on, nobody may approve a draft they prepared (created the preparation job, confirmed its intake, or saved it directly), drafts without a recorded preparer cannot be approved, and production posting approval and execution require that the current approval met the rule. A firm can instead require an independent reviewer **only for large drafts**: set a minimum, and any draft whose proposed entries total at least that amount (prepaid, accrual, owner and settlement entries, and a loan's entries together; an accrual's next-month reversal is not counted) needs a reviewer who did not prepare or confirm it. The comparison is in each draft's own currency. Every approval path applies the same check (individual, bulk, standing, approving a draft together with its posting, and production posting). Native QuickBooks corrections (Bill, expense and deposit line recodes, and payment and vendor credit applications) follow the same rule: whoever previewed the correction cannot approve it, and the rule is checked again when it is executed; under a review minimum, the line's or the application's amount is what counts, with the firm's row locked so a policy change cannot slip in between (migration 058). Each policy change is recorded with its reason, actor and time, together with the resulting independent review rule, review minimum, link passcode setting and materiality (migration 066). Materiality can be set or cleared by a hosted firm's org:manage holders and in the local workspace; see the tie-out in [Work inbox](work-inbox.md). The shared local development workspace cannot enable either rule; the database refuses it.

## Enabling accrual, loan, owner and Stripe fee posting

Deployment boundary: Go reads `PRODUCTION_POSTING_KINDS`, but Compose, the Cloudflare container adapters and staging workflow do not currently forward it. Native Go can receive the exported value; hosted activation requires wiring it through those layers first. Adding a GitHub secret alone has no effect.

These kinds stay off until `PRODUCTION_POSTING_KINDS` lists them (default `prepaid`), for example `prepaid,accrual,loan,owner,settlement`. Turn a kind on only after observing QuickBooks behavior in the sandbox company:

1. Post a test journal in the sandbox that touches each account type the kind uses: Expense and Liability for accruals and loan interest, Liability-to-Liability for current portion, Equity or Asset against Expense for owner items, Expense against Revenue (or the deposit account) for Stripe fee entries.
2. Read each account's `CurrentBalance` before and after. Verification expects debit-normal types (Asset, Expense) to rise on debits, and credit-normal types (Liability, Equity, Revenue) to rise on credits.
3. If QuickBooks doesn't move a balance this way, don't enable the kind: every readback would fail and leave postings unverified.

Posting reads every allowed account, checks the company and closing date, builds one journal, records each touched account's versioned balance, and verifies the journal lines and every balance movement after the write. Approval, independent review, basis, one durable attempt and uncertain-outcome rules are unchanged.

## Bill corrections and supplier credits in production

When a client's books are read from its production QuickBooks company, **Review a Bill expense-account correction** and **Reviewed supplier credit** write to that company instead of the sandbox. Each needs a current grant that covers the kind (**Bill expense corrections** or **Supplier credits**) and `bill_correction` or `vendor_credit` in `PRODUCTION_POSTING_KINDS`; they stay off until then. A Bill recode needs both the line's current and new expense accounts in the grant, and the line amount within the grant's maximum. A supplier credit needs the A/P account and the workpaper's prepaid account (found by name among the granted accounts) in the grant, the credit within the maximum and the workpaper in the client's currency. The preview records its grant; approval and the single attempt require that same grant, which is rechecked under the attempt lock. Verification of an earlier attempt uses the saved grant. Uncertain outcomes are never retried, as before.

**Expense and income deposit line recodes** (migration 064) use the same steps as a Bill recode: **Recode an expense line** for a card charge, check or cash expense (a QuickBooks Purchase) and **Recode an income deposit line** for a deposit. Each corrects one line's account under the accountant's cited coding rule, which is saved with the correction and bound into what is approved. An expense needs up to ten simple expense lines in the client's currency, no tax and nothing linked, and a new expense account. A deposit needs lines coded straight to an account (no deposited payments, no cash back, no tax), and both the line's current account and its new one must be income accounts. The write resends the transaction's lines exactly as QuickBooks returned them with only that account changed, and the readback requires a new version with every other field of the transaction and every other line unchanged. In production they need a grant covering **Expense line recodes** or **Income deposit line recodes** and `purchase_correction` or `deposit_correction` in `PRODUCTION_POSTING_KINDS`; they stay off until then. A Bill, an expense and a deposit with the same QuickBooks ID are separate corrections. Every correction, a Bill's included, now needs the rule reference.

**Credit applications** (migration 065): **Apply a payment or vendor credit** on the close pairs documents from the latest transaction capture's open items, under the accountant's cited rule. The agent suggests a pair when a payment's unapplied amount, or a vendor credit, equals exactly one open invoice or bill of the same party or its reference names exactly one by document number (a whole reference: INV-12 does not name INV-12-A); a document with two possible partners, a partner two documents would claim, and any document already in an application of the same company get no suggestion. A vendor credit names a bill only in its note, not by its own number. **Use** fills the form with the pair, amount and basis; the person adds the accountant's rule, confirms and previews as before, and a preview from a suggestion is refused if transactions were captured again since. Each unapplied payment and unused supplier credit in **Exceptions the agent found** links here (**Prepare a correction**). The form then opens with the agent's suggestion for that document. When there is no unambiguous suggestion (the document may match several, or be in an application already), the form opens with the document chosen and says so. When the document is no longer open in the latest capture, the form says that instead. A customer payment's unapplied amount is linked to an open invoice of the same customer: the payment is updated with its lines as read plus one line for the invoice. A vendor credit is applied to an open bill of the same vendor by a bill payment that moves no money (total zero, one line linking the bill and one the credit, a bank account named for QuickBooks, dated in the close month on or after both documents), sent once with a request ID. Whether QuickBooks accepts a zero-amount bill payment, and how it treats the request ID, is pending the sandbox check. The amount must be within what is open on both documents, which must be unchanged since the capture and in an open period. After the single attempt, both documents are read back and each open amount must have fallen by exactly the applied amount. The payment must keep every link it had and link the invoice for the applied amount more; the bill payment must link exactly the bill and the credit, pay nothing, and carry the approved date, bank account and currency. If a bill payment's create response is lost, a person can enter the bill payment ID found in QuickBooks; it is bound only if it passes the same readback, and nothing is sent again. An uncertain attempt blocks sign-off and close deletion. In production they need a grant covering **Customer payment applications** or **Vendor credit applications** and `payment_application` or `credit_application` in `PRODUCTION_POSTING_KINDS`; they stay off until then, the grant's maximum bounds the amount, and the bill payment's bank account must be one of the granted accounts.
