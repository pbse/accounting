# Supported prepaid and accrued-expense methods

These are explicit product methods, not universal accounting policy. The client's accounting professional decides whether each is appropriate, including materiality, credit treatment, prior-period treatment and reversals. Agents propose sourced facts; they cannot approve methods, invent estimates or post journals. All amounts are bounded nonnegative USD inputs with at most two decimal places. Calculations use integer cents; cash basis and foreign currencies remain unsupported.

## Start locally or on Cloudflare

Apply all migrations in numeric order with API and preparation worker stopped, then restart both on the same code revision. Fresh Compose volumes apply all migrations. No additional service or secret is required for these calculations. Existing hosted AuthKit organization/client permissions and database setup still apply. Cloudflare deployment must migrate the database before starting the new containers; API readiness checks for the required schema. This slice has local validation, not hosted activation evidence.

Open a client close. Upload terms and ledger/credit support, or use the synthetic examples in the preparation forms. Example buttons fill facts but never check approval boxes. Examples use February 2028; use a synthetic close for that month to save them. Avoid mixing synthetic examples into real client evidence.

The accountant can publish the method, conditions, materiality and supporting acceptance reference in **Approved procedures and client overrides**. Apply that reviewed version explicitly to the close. It is cited context, not automatic authorization. For each job, review proposed facts and selected sources, select the method, inspect the examples and confirm the booking assumptions. The durable job calculates a **pending draft**. The authorized accountant reviews the linked sources, amounts, assumptions and proposed adjustment, then records an approval/rejection with a reason in the existing review history. Editing a source invalidates approval; recalculation creates a fresh draft. A preparation checkbox is not final accountant sign-off.

## Partial-month prepaids

Choose one method explicitly:

- `equal_full_months_v1`: existing equal full-calendar-month allocation. Service begins on the first and ends on the last day of a month.
- `actual_days_v1`: invoice amount divided by the actual number of service days, including both endpoints. Each calendar month's amount uses its overlapping service days. Leap days count. Each monthly amount rounds down to cents; the final service month receives the residual.

Both are limited to 120 calendar months, with the close inside the service period. They do not infer taxes, useful lives or policy from descriptions.

Synthetic checks to review:

| Input | Expected expense |
| --- | --- |
| $100; September 1–November 30, 2026; equal months | September $33.33, October $33.33, November $33.34 |
| $100; January 16–March 15, 2028; actual days | January $26.66 (16 days), February $48.33 (29 days), March $25.01 (15 days) |
| $12,000; one service day in September 2026 | September $12,000, ending prepaid zero |

In the daily example, February's closing prepaid is $25.01. Without a supplied booked balance, the preparer must confirm the invoice was fully expensed with no prior entries or credits; the draft reclassifies that remaining balance from expense to prepaid.

## Credits and prior adjustments

The first supported credit treatment is **one prospective credit at the start of a selected service month** (or the service start if later). Supply a positive credit amount, effective month, credit/treatment reference and the current booked prepaid debit balance after the credit entry. The effective month must be inside the service period and no later than the close.

Earlier scheduled expense remains unchanged. Subtract the credit from the unamortized balance at that boundary, then allocate the remaining amount over the remaining months/days using the chosen method. A credit exceeding that balance is blocked. The schedule shows the credit separately; total scheduled expense plus credit equals the original invoice amount.

Example: apply a $10 credit effective February to the $100 daily example. January remains $26.66. The remaining $73.34 becomes $63.34 over 44 days: February $41.74, March $21.60. Total expense is $90.

For any prior adjustments, enter the prepaid balance actually booked through close and its ledger/cutoff reference. It is the current **debit balance**, not a proposed entry amount. The target minus booked balance determines the adjustment:

- February target $21.60, booked $30: debit expense / credit prepaid $8.40.
- Same target, booked $10: debit prepaid / credit expense $11.60.
- Same target, booked $21.60: no entry.

For multiple or retrospective credits, enter up to 20 individual positive credits, each with amount, effective service month, booked month and source reference. The booked month must be no earlier than the effective month and no later than this close. Cite the **client accountant's approved rule** and confirm the prepaid debit balance after all credit entries. Credits effective in an earlier service month revise the analytical schedule from that month; the calculator proposes only the current-close difference from the confirmed booked balance. It never edits an earlier close or posts a prior-period journal. The reviewer must resolve whether that treatment is acceptable before approving. Example: $120 January–March with $5 effective January but booked February and $10 effective/booked February yields $38.33, $33.33 and $33.34 of revised expense; total expense $105. At February close a booked prepaid balance of $40 yields a $6.66 debit expense / credit prepaid proposal. Excess credits, negative balances, combined credits/cancellation, mid-month cancellation and tax/foreign-currency effects remain blocked.

Confirm that net invoice cost is recorded between the selected expense/prepaid accounts and that the balance includes all prior entries. The calculator does not independently read or certify the ledger. The separate month-boundary cancellation treatments below do not change earlier expense.

## Month-boundary cancellation

The accountant chooses one of two explicit treatments for the effective close month. Both require a cancellation source, the current booked prepaid debit balance and ledger/cutoff reference. For a full refund, supply the balance after the refund entry; for no refund, supply the balance before any proposed write-off (or zero if already written off). They cannot be combined with a credit in one workpaper. Mid-month cancellation and corrections to earlier closes require separate review.

- **Full refund of the unamortized balance:** The schedule shows the remaining balance as a refund in the effective month and zero expense afterward. The refund must already be booked against prepaid, so the confirmed booked prepaid balance must be zero. This calculator does not create a cash or receivable entry.
- **No refund:** The schedule expenses the unamortized balance in the effective month and shows zero prepaid afterward. The booked balance must equal that entire unamortized balance (draft debit expense / credit prepaid) or be zero after an already-booked write-off. Partial prior write-offs need separate review; the calculator will not guess their period or treatment.

Synthetic example: $100 allocated equally over January–March 2028 produces January expense $33.33. A February full refund shows $66.67 refunded, February expense $0 and prepaid $0. A February cancellation without refund shows February expense $66.67 and prepaid $0; if the booked prepaid debit balance is $66.67, the proposed write-off is $66.67. Both examples require client-accountant approval before use.

## Deferred revenue and depreciation

Both use the prepaid schedule's math (equal full months or actual days, cents rounded down, residual in the final month) under the client's approved prepaid method; depreciation can instead use declining balance or the mid-month convention below. Open them from **Deferred revenue** or **Depreciation** on the close page.

- **Deferred revenue**: the amount billed in advance over the contract's service period, between a revenue account and a deferred revenue liability. The entry recognizes the earned month: 12,000 billed for September 2026–August 2027 with 12,000 booked proposes debit deferred revenue, credit revenue 1,000 at September 30. A blank booked balance means the invoice was booked straight to revenue, so the unearned part is deferred. Customer credits and cancellations work as they do for prepaids.
- **Depreciation**: straight-line depreciation of an asset's cost less any salvage value, between depreciation expense and accumulated depreciation. Equal months use the full-month convention (start on the first day of the in-service month); the end is the last day of the useful life, inclusive. The booked accumulated depreciation is required, including an explicit 0.00. A 3,600 laptop over September 2026–August 2029 depreciates 100.00 a month, or 90.00 with a 360 salvage value. Impairments are not supported.
- **Declining balance** (depreciation only, `declining_balance_v1`): each month takes the factor (2 or 1.5) ÷ the useful life in months of the net book value (cost less the schedule's depreciation to date), switching to straight-line over the remaining months once that is larger, and never taking book value below salvage. The same laptop at factor 2 depreciates 200.00, 188.88, 178.39… and from month 19 71.48 a month, with the 71.49 residual in the last month.
- **Mid-month convention** (depreciation, equal months or declining balance): the first month takes half a month and the month after the useful life ends takes the other half. The laptop depreciates 50.00 in September 2026, 100.00 a month to August 2029 and 50.00 in September 2029.

- **Asset disposal** (depreciation in equal months or declining balance): prepared in the close month of the sale or scrapping, with the proceeds (0.00 if scrapped), the disposal evidence and the asset cost, proceeds and gain or loss accounts (all different from each other and from accumulated depreciation). The schedule ends that month: no depreciation in the disposal month under the full-month convention, half a month under mid-month. Besides the usual true-up of booked accumulated depreciation, the draft proposes entries to book by hand: debit accumulated depreciation and debit proceeds up to book value, crediting the asset's cost in full; proceeds below book value debit the difference to the gain or loss account, and proceeds above it debit the excess to the proceeds account and credit it to the gain or loss account. The disposal month's remaining balance is zero. Book value is cost less the schedule's depreciation. The laptop sold for 1,000 in September 2027 has 1,200 accumulated: debit accumulated depreciation 1,200, cash 1,000 and loss on disposal 1,400; credit the laptop's cost 3,600. The preparer confirms the asset's cost is still booked, the booked accumulated depreciation is before the disposal and nothing for it has been recorded. Tie-out expects no accumulated depreciation for the asset once the entries are booked; the year-end handoff lists the entries. Partial disposals, impairments and trade-ins are not supported.

Declining balance, the mid-month convention and a disposal each require the client accountant's cited depreciation policy; an approved procedure does not itself approve a disposal. A client whose approved prepaid procedure names another method refuses them; the convention counts as part of the method, so a procedure approving equal full months refuses the mid-month convention too. A firm can approve these methods as a client's procedure through **Learn a reviewed method rule**: `declining_balance_v1`, `equal_full_months_v1+mid_month` and `declining_balance_v1+mid_month` for depreciation, and `pro_rata_service_days_v1` for accruals, each activated with an approved example that replays exactly.

Carry-forward and **Refresh recurring schedules** work for both, through the last month of the schedule (the month after the useful life under the mid-month convention); the refresh reads the client's DeferredRevenue or AccumulatedDepreciation account from the Trial Balance. Both stay draft-only: book the approved entry in the ledger.

## Fixed incurred accruals

`fixed_incurred_amount_v1` prepares a supported **closing accrued liability**, not an automatic estimate from a missing invoice. Supply service start/end, completed by close month-end; the evidence/approved estimation basis; target liability; current booked liability credit balance (explicit zero is allowed); ledger reference; and distinct expense/liability accounts.

`pro_rata_service_days_v1` accrues a service contract that may run past month-end: the target liability is the contract amount for the whole service period × the service days elapsed through month-end ÷ all its service days (both inclusive, cents rounded down), less what the vendor has invoiced to date and is booked outside the accrued liability (an explicit 0.00 when nothing is). It needs the accountant's cited rule and a service start by month-end; invoiced above the elapsed share is refused as a prepaid. A 9,000 maintenance contract for August 15–November 14, 2026 has 47 of 92 days elapsed at September 30: 4,597.82 incurred; with 3,000 invoiced, the target is 1,597.82, and a booked 1,000 gives a 597.82 proposal. Enter the contract and invoiced amounts in place of the target, which is always recomputed. The service period is limited to ten years. It is not carried forward or refreshed automatically, and it is prepared from the accrual form rather than Work intake.

Select whether to propose a reversal on the first of the following month. It reverses **only the proposed adjustment**, not the entire target liability or prior entries. Reversals are draft entries, not scheduled execution.

Example: supported liability $1,200, booked $200 → debit expense / credit liability $1,000 at month-end. Optional next-month reversal: debit liability / credit expense $1,000. Booked $1,400 → correct downward by $200. Booked $1,200 → no adjustment or reversal.

`actual_invoice_true_up_v1` links a prior estimate amount/reference to a distinct actual invoice reference and the client accountant's approved rule. The entered amount is the approved **current closing liability**, supported by the actual invoice; the entered booked balance already reflects invoices, payments, reversals and earlier adjustments through this close. The workpaper reports actual minus estimate as a variance. Only current target minus booked liability becomes a proposed adjustment, and optional next-month reversal applies only to that new adjustment. Example: prior estimate $1,200, actual closing liability $1,250, booked liability $1,200 → variance +$50 and debit expense / credit liability $50. If the booked liability is already $1,250, no entry or reversal is proposed. This does not automatically derecognize paid invoices or revise an earlier period.

The local agent recognizes exact labeled facts. To propose an accrual locally, put `Preparation kind: accrual` on its own line in the primary source, followed by labels such as `Amount:`, `Service start:`, `Service end:`, `Currency:`, `Expense account:`, `Liability account:`, `Booked balance:`, `Booking reference:` and `Estimation basis:`. This classifies a proposal only. OpenAI interpretation supports the same fields when configured, each citing a quote that must occur once in the source and print the extracted value. Neither path accepts approval flags from document text; conflicting candidates remain visible for review.

## Loan schedules

**Loan schedule** (`/dashboard/loans/new`, method `lender_statement_v1`) checks one month of a loan against the lender's statement.

- **The lender's split is used as stated.** Each payment's principal and interest come from the statement. Interest is recomputed from the note's rate and exact day-count (Actual/360, Actual/365, 30/360 bond basis or 30E/360) on the principal outstanding for (previous paid-through date, payment date]. Any difference becomes a question ("check for a rate change, fees or a different day-count"); the split is never changed.
- **The statement must tie.** If opening principal less principal paid does not equal the lender's closing principal, the draft is blocked and no entry is proposed.
- **A book-vs-lender difference is a question, never an entry.** Nothing proves the difference is interest; the preparer finds the cause first.
- **Proposed entries, dated month-end, never posted:**
  - Accrued interest from the last paid-through date to month-end on the closing principal, less the booked accrued interest, when the preparer chooses to accrue (accrual-basis clients).
  - Current portion reclassification to the principal due in the next 12 months (capped at the balance), from the lender's amortization schedule, only when the preparer confirms this is a reclassification month and the loan and current portion accounts together equal the lender's balance.
- **Per-client enablement.** Loan drafts save only when the client's effective procedure (a firm default or a client override under **Approved procedures and client overrides**) enables loan schedules with this method. A disabled client override stops new loan drafts for that client even when the firm default is on. The procedure's instructions carry the firm's cadence and fee treatment.
- **Rate changes and fees added to the balance** (method `lender_statement_variable_v1`, a separately approved loan procedure). Each rate change needs an effective date after the paid-through date and by month-end, in order, and the lender's notice; the new rate applies from its effective date. Each fee needs a date in the close month, a positive amount, a description and its statement line; it is added to the balance at the end of that day, after any payment that day, and bears interest from the next day. Expected interest for each window is summed over the rate and principal segments and rounded once. The statement ties as opening less principal paid plus fees. Fees not yet booked to the loan are proposed as debit fee expense, credit loan (reversed if more was booked); the book-vs-lender question counts that proposal. A draft with either needs the variable method (or the issuance cost method below), and the save needs that method approved for the client. Compounding is not supported.
- **Rates from an index** (same methods). For a rate change set from SOFR or EFFR, the preparer chooses the index, enters the note's margin and picks a date. The app reads the New York Fed's published value on or in the ten days before that date (no key needed) and stores it with the date it was published for. The rate is that value plus the margin, and the notice or note clause is still cited. The draft is refused if its index value is not the published one on record when it is checked, saved or run from a queued job. A revised publication replaces the stored value; a draft already saved keeps the value it was saved with, and approving it does not look the rate up again. Floors, caps, averages of SOFR over a period, Prime and Term SOFR are entered by hand with the notice.
- **Fees paid in cash with a payment** (same methods). A fee the client paid with a payment, not added to the balance, is entered on that payment with its statement line; an amount without a statement line, or a line without a positive amount, is refused. It changes neither principal nor interest, and this draft proposes no entry for it; check separately how the fee was booked. The schedule shows it, with the month's total. When the ledger's loan balance is lower than the lender's by exactly those fees, the book-difference question names them as a possible cause (booked against principal) and asks to check the statement before moving them.
- **Debt issuance costs** (method `lender_statement_costs_v1`, its own approved loan procedure, which also covers rate changes and fees). Costs of obtaining the loan are kept in their own account, deducted from the debt, so the loan account still holds principal and ties to the lender. They are amortized to the loan's interest account straight-line over the term, in equal full months or actual days (up to 120 months), with the prepaid schedule's rounding. That needs the accountant's cited conclusion that straight-line is not materially different from the interest method the standard describes. The preparer confirms the costs were booked to that account and gives its month-end balance with a ledger reference; the difference from the unamortized schedule is proposed (debit interest, credit the issuance cost account, or the reverse when more was amortized). The draft shows the month's amortization, the total to date and the unamortized costs at month-end. Cash and modified-cash clients are refused. Effective-interest amortization, costs netted inside the loan account, modifications and extinguishment are not supported.
- Drafts with any proposed entry are reviewed individually, never in bulk.

## Owner-related reclassifications

**Owner item** (`/dashboard/owners/new`, method `owner_reclass_v1`) handles one owner-related transaction in the close month.

- **An unanswered purpose stays a question.** Choose "Not yet" to save a specific client question with a blocker and no entry (PRD Appendix F).
- **An answer becomes one proposed entry, dated month-end.** Record what the client said and where, and name the booked and owner accounts.
  - The business paid a personal cost: debit the owner account (draw or distribution, or "Due from owner" with a cited repayment confirmation), credit the account it was booked to, for the personal share (100% − business use).
  - The owner paid a business cost: debit the expense, credit the owner account (contribution, or "Due to owner" with a cited reimbursement confirmation), for the business share. The ledger must show it isn't already booked or reimbursed.
- **The entity type decides the owner accounts.** It is recorded per client under **Owner treatments**; a firm default carries only policy and account names. Drafts must match the client's recorded type. Account names are suggested per type (Owner's draw, Partner draws, Shareholder distributions, Dividends paid) and stay editable.
  - A C corporation distribution needs a cited dividend declaration.
  - A partnership draw or contribution needs the partner and that partner's account.
- Earlier periods need the firm's prior-period approval and aren't handled here. Tax treatment (constructive dividends, basis tracking, reasonable salary) is never decided. Nothing is posted.

## Reviewing a draft

A draft's page keeps its evidence beside the calculation, the proposed entry and the review decision:

- **Evidence as received** highlights the cited lines of the extracted text. A PDF citation "page P, line L" is counted from that page, so the highlight lands on the right line. **View original, page P** opens the original PDF inline at that page in the browser's own viewer; the extracted text beside it shows the highlighted line.
- **Since your last review** (`GET /v1/closes/{id}/workpapers/{paper}/since-review`) finds your latest decision on this draft or any draft it revises (the whole chain). It shows what changed since that draft and why review reopened, naming only reasons that apply now: a newer draft replaces this one, it was revised since your review (and the drafts in between), a linked source is gone, changed or was replaced (with the replacement and its reason), the schedule it carries from is no longer the approved version it was built from, or someone requested changes after your review. The comparison with the direct predecessor stays below it.
- **Comments** (migration 077, `GET`/`POST .../comments`) are questions anchored to the whole draft, one of its inputs, or a line of one of its linked sources (click the line number in the evidence). They go only on the current, unapproved draft; to question an approved draft, request changes first. A draft shows its own comments and those on the drafts it revises, each linked to the draft it was made on. Anyone who can open the close resolves a comment with a note. While any comment on the draft or its earlier versions is open, the draft cannot be approved, by hand, in bulk, with its posting or by a standing approval; comments and approvals take the close's lock, so neither can slip past the other.

## Review, carry-forward and posting boundaries

Saved drafts retain method, example confirmation, credit/balance references, selected evidence revisions and any reversal proposal. The ZIP package includes their inputs and calculation outputs; prepaid CSV adds a credit column. Source changes invalidate reviews through the same existing path.

Reviewed prepaid schedules can carry forward as schedules only. They retain source lineage, clear confirmations and never claim a fresh ledger balance or propose a carried journal. A reviewed fixed recurring accrual can start the next month's draft only after the accountant selects current service evidence, confirms the exact current QuickBooks Trial Balance liability row and its booked balance, and reconfirms the approved rule and reversal choice. The prior workpaper remains linked; a changed prior review reopens dependent review. The product does not execute accrual reversals. New daily/credit/balance-adjusted/cancellation prepaid methods and all accruals are draft/review only; the existing full-month sandbox journal path is not extended to them.

For a reviewed supplier credit in the current close, a separate **VendorCredit** preview maps one cited source and exact 3–21 character credit reference to a QuickBooks sandbox vendor, A/P account and prepaid asset account. The accountant confirms the $25 synthetic example (debit A/P, credit prepaid asset), verifies the vendor name and that the credit has not already been booked or included in the booked prepaid balance, reviews the exact preview, approves it for ten minutes and executes once. The app checks QuickBooks for an existing VendorCredit with that document number and blocks another local attempt for the same reference; credits entered under another number still need accountant reconciliation. After posting, refresh the ledger and workpaper before sign-off. An uncertain provider result blocks retry; use canonical readback and investigate any mismatch. It requires a hosted role with separate posting approval/execution permissions. Production VendorCredit uses a separate client grant and the controls in [production posting](production-posting.md); connecting a production company alone grants no write. Cash refunds, tax and prior-period corrections remain unsupported.

For an **uncategorized expense** or **inconsistent coding** exception, refresh QuickBooks transactions, open **Review a Bill expense-account correction**, select a captured Bill and one line, then record the client accountant's reason and exact target expense account ID. The supported native operation changes one selected account-based expense line on an unpaid USD Bill with at most ten simple lines in the configured sandbox. Preview checks the entire live Bill against the latest completed capture. An authorized accountant approves the exact preview for ten minutes; an executor makes one version-bound update and verifies canonical readback. Changed, paid, linked, taxed, prior-period, or otherwise unsupported Bills stop for manual handling. A new capture resets an unused approval; an uncertain write blocks capture replacement and any retry. Refresh the capture and dependent work after a verified change. Production Bill writes use a separate client-specific grant and connector validation; see [production posting](production-posting.md).

Synthetic review example: a $100 unpaid Bill dated in the current close has a $60 expense line coded to account 7 and a $40 expense line coded to account 8. The client's accountant confirms only the $40 line belongs to active account 9. The proposed correction changes that line's account reference to 9 and sends both lines in the version-bound sandbox update. Vendor, date, total, payable balance, descriptions, line IDs, both amounts and the $60 line's account must read back unchanged. Bills with linked payments, taxes, non-expense lines, more than ten lines, or an uncertain write stay blocked. The accountant must approve this exact treatment and example before use; production writes require separate activation and grants.

Xero bills take the same treatment through **Recode a Xero bill line**: one line of an approved bill in the close month with nothing paid or credited, no tax and up to ten lines moves to another expense account, under the client's Xero grant and with the same preview, independent approval, single attempt and exact readback. It stays off until the operator checks the update on a Xero demo company; see [Xero bill corrections](xero-onboarding.md#bill-corrections).

## Card reconciliation and reviewed timing or clearing

Choose `card_liability_month_v1` for a USD credit-card liability and apply the client accountant's approved reconciliation procedure to the close. Source-linked preparation requires that procedure as evidence; the server records its version as the card rule. A newly published enabled version does not replace a close's pinned version until explicitly applied; a disabled effective procedure stops new card preparation. Normalize **both** the card statement and ledger CSV: charges increase the positive liability; payments and credits decrease it. Opening plus signed movement must equal closing independently on each source. Dates, page inventory, exact match candidates, groups and tolerances use the existing reconciliation review rules. The preparer cannot treat an opposite-sign match as settled, manufacture a balancing entry or mark native QuickBooks reconciliation complete.

For an unmatched signed row, choose the **timing** treatment only when source records show the same transaction belongs on the opposite side in another period. Choose **clearing** only with separately linked clearing-account evidence for the unsettled amount; source-linked preparation replaces the typed reference with the selected evidence location. Both need a cited accountant-approved rule and a reviewer reason. A current classified item bridges the closing difference; an approved prior item bridges opening until an opposite-source row with the exact same signed amount clears it. The next month's workpaper carries the item and citation for fresh review, without a journal or automatic settlement.

Synthetic card example: September opens at $0. A $100 charge and $25 payment give a $75 card statement liability; the ledger also includes a $10 charge that will appear on a later statement, so its liability is $85. A reviewed ledger-only clearing item of +$10 bridges September closing to zero. October opens with statement $75 and ledger $85; the cited +$10 card statement row clears the prior item and both close at $85. The accountant must approve the card sign rule, clearing source, and this example before the real workpaper is approved. A missing page, incomplete source, mismatched sign, or unexplained amount remains blocked.
